INSTRUCTIONS FOR POLISHING THESE AUDIOBOOK SCRIPTS
===================================================

Read this whole file before you change a single word of a book.
The books are already complete. Your job is to make them speak cleanly
in the app described below. Your job is not to shorten them, retell them,
modernise them, or "improve" the author's sentences.


WHO THIS IS FOR
---------------

These files are narration scripts for a local Windows app called
AI Video Generation Manager, channel name Mighty Chapters.

The person who sends you this file will also send one or more book files
from the book-scripts folder. Polish each book separately. Return one
plain text file per book, UTF-8, same filename.

The three full books to polish, if they are attached, are:

  dracula-bram-stoker-full-audiobook.txt
  frankenstein-mary-shelley-full-audiobook.txt
  the-picture-of-dorian-gray-oscar-wilde-full-audiobook.txt

They are the full public-domain novels (Project Gutenberg text), already
split into spoken chapters. They are not summaries. Do not turn them
into summaries.


HOW THE APP TURNS THE FILE INTO AN AUDIOBOOK
--------------------------------------------

There is no PDF step. Do not format the script like a PDF, a screenplay,
a subtitle file, or a webpage.

The app does this, in order:

  1. It reads the text file.
  2. A line that starts with a hash and a space (# ) is a chapter title.
     That title is spoken out loud, and it becomes a YouTube chapter.
  3. A blank line separates paragraphs.
  4. The app cleans the text a second time (see "The second pass" below),
     then adds its own short Introduction and Closing if you left those out.
  5. A voice engine speaks the text into WAV, then FFmpeg joins it into
     one MP3 (audiobook.mp3, 96k or 128k, speech).
  6. The words are timed. An MP4 video is drawn on top of the MP3:
     a still background, a ring that moves with the voice, and live
     captions. Optional mode: one still card per chapter.
  7. It also writes a thumbnail JPG, an SRT, a description, and tags.

Engines, set by the person running the app, not by markup in the file:

  piper:en_US-joe-medium     local Piper. One plain voice for the whole
                             book. No emotion controls.
  kokoro:bm_george           local Kokoro. Also one plain voice.
                             Other Kokoro voices exist (am_michael,
                             af_heart, hm_omega) but the file cannot
                             switch voice mid-book.
  edge:en-US-AndrewMultilingualNeural
                             Microsoft Edge, online. Still plain text.
                             Edge can do Urdu and Hindi
                             (ur-PK-AsadNeural, hi-IN-MadhurNeural)
                             but these three books are English.

Speed is one number for the whole book (about 0.5 to 2.0). You cannot
mark one paragraph slow and the next fast.

There is no emotion track. You cannot mark a line whispered, angry,
crying, or scared. The voice does not read SSML. It does not read
stage directions. Emotion is carried only by the words that are already
in the novel and by normal punctuation.


THE SECOND PASS (WHY YOUR MARKUP GETS DESTROYED)
------------------------------------------------

After you return the file, the app runs it through a cleaner before
the voice speaks. That cleaner will:

  - Turn an em dash, or two hyphens (--), into a comma.
  - Delete these characters wherever they appear: [ ] { } < > | *
  - Turn Mr. into Mister, Mrs. into Missus, Dr. into Doctor,
    St. into Saint when a name follows, Prof. into Professor.
  - Turn a word of four or more CAPITAL letters into Title Case
    (DRACULA becomes Dracula). Roman numerals are left alone.
  - Collapse repeated spaces and spaces before punctuation.

So if you add any of the following, it will be deleted or spoken as
garbage. Do not add them:

  [sad]  [pause]  [whisper]  (softly)  *emphasis*
  <break time="1s"/>  <prosody>  any other SSML or XML
  **bold**  _italic_  markdown links
  ALL CAPS shouting
  emoji

A previous edit removed colons. That broke the reading. Do not do it
again. The rules for punctuation are below.


PUNCTUATION YOU MUST KEEP
-------------------------

Period .     Ends a sentence. The app splits captions and, on Kokoro
             and Edge, speech chunks at the period. Keep it.

Question ?   Ends a sentence. The voice lifts. Keep it.

Exclamation !  Ends a sentence. Keep it. Do not sprinkle new ones in.

Comma ,      A short pause. Also a place where a caption may break
             when a sentence is longer than about 180 characters.
             Keep the author's commas.

Semicolon ;  A pause. Also a legal caption break. Keep it.

Colon :      A pause, not the end of a sentence. Also a legal caption
             break. KEEP EVERY COLON.

             This is the mistake that ruined the last pass. A line like

               my hopes fluctuate, and my spirits are often depressed.
               I am about to proceed on a long and difficult voyage,
               the emergencies of which will demand all my fortitude:
               I am required not only to raise the spirits of others

             must keep the colon. Deleting it glues two clauses into
             one breath and removes a caption break.

             Also keep colons in times, labels, and dialogue leads
             ("Jonathan Harker's Journal:", "he said:"). If a colon is
             only there because a heading was pasted into a paragraph,
             you may turn that one into a period. Do not hunt colons.

Quotation marks   Keep them around spoken dialogue. Straight "quotes"
                  are safer than fancy ones. Do not delete the quotes
                  and do not turn dialogue into reported speech.

Apostrophes  Keep them. father's, don't, Harker's. Do not strip them.

Ellipsis     Prefer one single ellipsis character (…) or one period.
             Three dots in a row (...) get split as three sentence
             endings and the voice stumbles. If you see ..., replace
             that one cluster with a single period or a single ….


CHAPTER LINES
-------------

A chapter line is a line by itself that starts with "# " (hash, space).
The words after that are spoken as the chapter title. Keep them short
enough to say in one breath, ideally under 80 characters.

Good:

  # Chapter One. Jonathan Harker's Journal

Bad:

  # CHAPTER ONE: JONATHAN HARKER'S JOURNAL -- continued!!
  ## Chapter One
  Chapter One
  **Chapter One**

Rules:

  - Use one hash, then a space. Never ## or ###. A double hash becomes
    its own extra chapter.
  - One chapter heading, then a blank line, then the prose.
  - Do not merge chapters. Do not split one chapter into many unless
    the heading is broken (see the repair list).
  - Do not add a table of contents. The app builds the chapter list
    from these lines.
  - Spell titles the way a person says them. In a heading the app does
    NOT expand Dr. or Mr., so write Doctor and Mister in headings.

    Change "Dr. Seward's Diary" to "Doctor Seward's Diary".
    Change "JOURNAL--continued" to "Journal, continued".
    The double hyphen would be spoken, or, if the cleaner touches it
    later, turned into a comma in the wrong place. Write the comma
    yourself so the heading is already speakable.

  - Headings are spoken. Do not put a heading that is only a number,
    a date stamp, or a joke.


PARAGRAPHS
----------

Paragraphs are blocks of prose separated by one blank line.
Do not put a blank line after every sentence. That makes hundreds of
tiny chapters-of-silence and a choppy voice.
Do not glue the whole chapter into one paragraph either. Keep the
paragraph breaks that are already there.


WHAT YOU ARE ALLOWED TO CHANGE
------------------------------

Only repairs that stop the voice saying nonsense. Examples that are
already in these files and should be fixed:

  1. Broken years. The cleaner once turned a double hyphen into a comma,
     so Frankenstein has lines like:

        Saint Petersburgh, Dec. 11th, 17,.
        Archangel, 28th March, 17,.

     The novel printed the year as 17-- on purpose (the year is blank).
     Repair the sense without inventing a year:

        Saint Petersburg, December the eleventh, year left blank.
        Archangel, the twenty-eighth of March, year left blank.

     Search the file for ",." and for a dangling comma that used to be
     a dash. Repair those. Do not invent plot.

  2. Headings that contain "--" or the word JOURNAL in all caps.
     Make them speakable, as described above.

  3. Leftover Gutenberg noise if any survived: the words
     "Illustration", a lone footnote number, "[deleted]", a line that
     is only underscores, or a line of page furniture.
     Delete the noise. Do not delete the sentence it was attached to.

  4. A word the voice will misread, repaired by a normal respelling
     that still means the same thing:
        Dec.      -> December
        11th      -> the eleventh
        St.       -> Saint   (in a place name or a saint's name)
        Mr.       -> Mister
        Mrs.      -> Missus
        Dr.       -> Doctor
        No.       -> Number   (only when it means a number, not the word no)
        etc.      -> and so on
        e.g.      -> for example
        i.e.      -> that is
     Do this in prose and in headings.

  5. A sentence with no ending period at the end of a paragraph.
     Add the period so the voice does not run into the next paragraph.

That is the whole list. If a sentence is old-fashioned but grammatical,
leave it. Mary Shelley, Bram Stoker, and Oscar Wilde stay in their
own words.


WHAT YOU MUST NOT CHANGE
------------------------

  - Do not summarise. Do not cut scenes, letters, diaries, or descriptions.
  - Do not add introductions, recaps, "in this chapter", morals,
    jokes, modern comments, or author biography inside the book.
  - Do not add sound effects, music cues, or narrator asides.
  - Do not reorder chapters.
  - Do not replace a long sentence with a shorter one of your own.
  - Do not remove colons, semicolons, commas, question marks,
    exclamation marks, or quotation marks as a style cleanup.
  - Do not convert the book to a bullet list, a table, JSON, or HTML.
  - Do not translate. These three stay in English.
  - The word count of each file must stay within about 2 percent of
    the original, aside from the small repairs above. If your file is
    much shorter, you deleted the book. Put it back.


INTRODUCTION AND CLOSING
------------------------

The files as sent already start with "# Introduction" and end with
"# Closing". Those two blocks were added by the app.

Delete those two blocks in your output, and delete only those blocks:

  - Delete from the first line through the blank line after
    "Sit back, relax, and enjoy."
  - Delete the final "# Closing" heading and the thank-you paragraph
    under it.

The app will add a fresh Introduction and Closing when the audiobook
is built. If you leave them in, the listener hears the introduction
twice and the closing twice.

Do not write a new introduction of your own. The first line of your
file must be the first real chapter heading, for example:

  # Letter One

  # Chapter One. Jonathan Harker's Journal

  # Chapter One


OUTPUT
------

For each book, return one UTF-8 text file.

  - Same chapter count as the source, after the Introduction and
    Closing blocks are removed.
  - Unix or Windows line endings are both fine.
  - No byte-order mark needed.
  - No markdown fence around the book.
  - No comment at the top. The first line is a chapter heading.

Before you finish, check:

  [ ] Colons are still in the file. Count them. If the count dropped
      sharply, you made the old mistake. Restore them.
  [ ] No line contains [ ] { } < > | * or a double hyphen.
  [ ] No SSML, no emotion tags, no stage directions.
  [ ] Every chapter heading is one line starting with "# ".
  [ ] "# Introduction" and "# Closing" are gone.
  [ ] The story was not shortened.


FOR THE PERSON RUNNING THE APP (DO NOT READ THIS ALOUD)
-------------------------------------------------------

After the polished file is back, build the audiobook from the project
folder with:

  .\.venv\Scripts\python scripts\make_book.py --text book-scripts\dracula-bram-stoker-full-audiobook.txt --title "Dracula" --author "Bram Stoker" --kind public-domain --voice piper:en_US-joe-medium --plan-only

--plan-only prints the chapter list and the time estimate. It does not
speak. If the chapter list looks right, run the same command without
--plan-only.

Use --kind public-domain for these three. That makes the spoken opening
say "complete, unabridged audiobook". Do not use --kind summary and do
not use --kind original.

Swap the filename, title, and author for Frankenstein (Mary Shelley)
and The Picture of Dorian Gray (Oscar Wilde).

Dracula is about 18 hours of speech. YouTube allows about 12 hours per
upload, so that video has to be split into two parts after it is made.
Frankenstein is about 8 hours. Dorian Gray is about 9 hours.

A long animated 1080p video is huge. For these books, add
--height 720 or --video cards so the render fits on the disk.
The MP3 is the audiobook. The MP4 is the picture track on top of it.
There is no PDF.
