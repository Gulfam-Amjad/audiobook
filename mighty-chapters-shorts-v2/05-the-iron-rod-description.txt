TITLE
The Iron Rod That Went Through a Man's Head, and He Lived

DESCRIPTION
On September 13, 1848, Phineas Gage was a railroad foreman in Vermont. An explosion drove an iron tamping rod through his left cheek and out the top of his skull. He survived, but friends said he was no longer himself. Doctors studied him for years. His case became an early clue that personality depends on the brain. He died in 1860. The rod is kept at Harvard.
